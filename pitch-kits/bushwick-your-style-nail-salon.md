## 2. In person

### In person — 45 seconds, then show the screen

**Open with the observation, not with yourself.**

> "Hi — quick question. If I search *nail salon* in Brooklyn on my phone right now, Bushwick Your Style Nail Salon comes up.
> You have 3.8 stars from 154 reviews, which is really good. But there's no website on your
> listing — Google is literally showing an 'Add missing information' button. Every customer I send your
> way ends up on a competitor's site instead of yours."

**Wait. Let them respond. Do not talk over the pause.**

**Then show the screen.** Open the proposal on your phone or tablet:

> "I built you this on Tuesday night. It's a working site, not a picture. It's got your photos, your
> reviews, your hours, one-tap calling. Have a look — tell me what's wrong with it."

**Handle it as a working session, not a pitch.** If they change something, change it in front of them.
That is the whole trick: the site is already real, so you are collaborating, not selling.

**The close — ask for the smaller commitment:**

> "So it's $900 for Starter, $1,150 for Growth, $1,400 for Pro. I only take two builds a week, and
> I've got one slot left this month. A $450 deposit locks it in and I have the first draft in front of
> you in 48 hours. Which one do you want?"

**If they hesitate:**

> "Totally fair. How about this — I put the first draft together, you look at it, and if it's not right
> you don't pay the rest. The only thing I need up front is $450 to cover the work I've already done."

**Leave the mock-up with them.** It is the only thing that closes this later.

## 3. Phone

### Phone — 30 seconds, permission first

**Open (do not ask "is this the owner?" first):**

> "Hi, is this Bushwick Your Style Nail Salon? My name is Your Name — I build websites for local businesses. I was
> looking at Bushwick Your Style Nail Salon on Google and I had a quick question. Have you got 30 seconds?"

**If yes — the finding:**

> "Right, so Bushwick Your Style Nail Salon is rated 3.8 from 154 reviews on Google, which is genuinely strong. But
> your listing has no website on it, and Google is showing an 'Add missing information' prompt where
> your site should be. That means people who search for nail salon in Brooklyn, like what they see,
> and then have nowhere to go."

**Diagnose with two questions:**

1. "Where do most of your new customers come from at the moment — word of mouth, or people Googling you?"
2. "If someone Googled you right now and there was a proper site on your listing, would that change
   how many calls you get?"

**The demonstration:**

> "I actually built you a mock-up. It uses your own photos and your own reviews. Let me send it to you
> right now so you can click around on it — it's a working site, not a picture."

*(send the proposal link while still on the call)*

**The close:**

> "So it's $900 for Starter, $1,150 for Growth, $1,400 for Pro. Fixed price, live in 7 to 14 days,
> you own it outright. I only take two builds a week — I have one slot left this month. A $450 deposit
> holds it and I have a first draft in front of you within 48 hours. Can I put you down for it?"

**If they want to think about it:**

> "Of course. I'll email the mock-up over now so you can show it to your partner — and I'll check in
> Thursday. Can I put a 20-minute call in the diary for Thursday afternoon? If it's a no, no hard
> feelings, and I'll leave you alone."

*(Confirm the follow-up date and time in the call, then write it in the CRM.)*

---

### Do not
- Never use an auto-dialer or dial from a list of scraped numbers. Dial by hand, during business hours.
- Do not pretend to be a customer or a directory. Say who you are and why you are calling.
- Do not quote a price before the mock-up has been sent to them.

### If they do not reply at all
Move them to the monthly re-check list. Every few weeks their listing gets more reviews, their
competitor launches a site, or their rating dips. Those three events are a legitimate reason to reach
out again — and a new reason to reach out, not the same email.
